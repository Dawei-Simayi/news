# WestJet Flight Attendants Ratify New Collective Agreement

## 1. Title
WestJet Ratifies New Labor Agreement, Concluding Strike Actions

## 2. Core Thesis
WestJet's recent ratification of a new collective agreement with its 4,400 flight attendants ensures operational stability following a disruptive strike. The agreement, featuring an 18% wage increase over three years and compensation for previously unpaid ground work, aligns WestJet with evolving industry labor standards (similar to American and Delta) but structurally increases operating expenses.

## 3. Key Catalysts
- **Operational Normalization:** End of the strike allows the resumption of full capacity utilizing Boeing 737 and 787 fleets, recovering lost revenue momentum from the holiday weekend disruptions.
- **Labor Precedent:** Implementation of boarding and ground pay sets a new standard within the Canadian market, potentially pressuring other regional competitors to follow suit.

## 4. Downside Risks
- **Margin Compression:** The >18% wage hike over three years, coupled with the new ground pay provisions, will significantly elevate structural CASM (Cost Available Seat Mile) and could compress margins if costs cannot be fully passed on to consumers.
- **Strike Hangover:** Short-term reputational damage and lost bookings from the recent holiday weekend disruptions might take time to fully normalize.

## 5. Valuation
The new agreement provides revenue predictability by avoiding further labor unrest but places downward pressure on earnings estimates due to higher labor costs. Valuation multiples may face slight contraction in the near term until management demonstrates the ability to offset these costs through yield management or efficiency gains.

## 6. Market Sentiment
Sentiment is mixed but leaning positive on the certainty provided by the resolution. While investors will appreciate the removal of the strike overhang, there will be cautious monitoring of the margin implications of the new cost structure.

## 7. Competitive Landscape
As Canada's second-largest airline, WestJet's labor cost increase could reduce its cost advantage against Air Canada, while simultaneously pressuring ultra-low-cost carriers in the region if they are forced to match these compensation standards to retain talent. 

## 8. Supply Chain / Macro Impact
The resolution allows full utilization of Boeing 737 and 787 aircraft, stabilizing the related aviation supply chain and airport operations across WestJet's 100+ destinations. In the broader macro context, the 18% wage increase highlights persistent wage inflation pressures within the services and transportation sectors.

## 9. Conclusion
The ratification of the collective agreement is a necessary clearing event for WestJet, removing the immediate threat of operational paralysis. However, the resulting structural cost increases will require disciplined capacity and yield management to preserve profitability. The move aligns WestJet with global peers on cabin crew compensation, ensuring labor stability at the cost of near-term margin pressure.
